System goals

SG-01 Start a session from a personal device with no installation or account

The system shall let a visitor reach a working input surface by scanning a code, with no app, login, or staff assistance.
Success criteria (qualitative): From scan to first submitted turn requires no step other than consent and typing.
Satisfies: BG-02 Give visitors direct, unassisted first-hand experience

SG-02 Make turn-taking readable from across the room

The system shall present recent exchanges on a projected surface at a size and pacing that allows a standing observer to read a complete visitor contribution and its Sparring reply.
Success criteria (qualitative): A complete exchange is legible at typical viewing distance in the exhibition space. Exact typography and layout: resolved (masonry, run unchanged through the exhibition — SE-02 TBC-01).
Satisfies: BG-01 Make Sparring’s friction visible to an audience

SG-03 Survive interruption of the visitor’s session

The system shall let a visitor return to their session after a page reload, a locked phone, or a backgrounded browser, without losing prior turns.
Success criteria (qualitative): Reloading the input surface restores the session and its history.
Satisfies: BG-02 Give visitors direct, unassisted first-hand experience
Rationale: Exhibition devices are fidgeted with. A session lost to a reload is indistinguishable in the record from a visitor who gave up.

SG-04 Run unattended and absorb misuse without operator intervention

The system shall limit request volume per visitor, cap session length and input size, and continue functioning when individual requests are rejected.
Success criteria (qualitative): No failure mode requires someone in the room to restore the projection.
Satisfies: BG-03 Run unattended through exhibition hours

SG-05 Record every interaction with its origin fixed at creation

The system shall persist sessions and exchanges tagged as pilot or live at the moment they are created.
Success criteria (qualitative): Live exhibition material can be extracted without manual sorting.
Satisfies: BG-04 Produce a record of public interactions as secondary

SG-06 Never present an empty surface

When no live session is available for display, the system shall present pilot material instead.
Success criteria (qualitative): The projection is populated at all times while running.
Satisfies: BG-01 Make Sparring’s friction visible to an audience, BG-03 Run unattended through exhibition hours

SG-07 Gate public projection at the moment of submission, not after the fact

The system shall reject a contribution assessed as unsuitable before it reaches the Sparring partner or the display feed, rather than storing it and hiding it later.
Success criteria (qualitative): An unsuitable contribution never becomes an exchange; the visitor sees that it was rejected and can edit and resubmit; the session itself continues.
Satisfies: BG-01 Make Sparring’s friction visible to an audience
Supports: BQR-03 Nothing identifying a visitor reaches the projection.
Rationale: Gating at submission rather than at display means nothing unsuitable is ever written down in the first place — there is no later step where it could leak. This also means projection eligibility for a stored exchange no longer needs a moderation-driven flag of its own: everything stored has already passed the check by construction, so what’s actually projected is governed by the visitor’s own projection consent (see SC-05 Retention requires consent; projection requires), not by re-deciding suitability at read time.

SG-08 Hold a pedagogical position under sustained pressure or premature-closure attempts

The system shall keep the Sparring partner’s challenge substantive — not resolved by a confident-sounding answer, capitulation to pressure, or a handed-over conclusion — for the duration of a session.
Success criteria (qualitative): A visitor who pressures, argues, or sounds satisfied without addressing the weak point still receives further challenge, not agreement or closure.
Satisfies: BG-01 Make Sparring’s friction visible to an audience
Supports: BQR-01 Visitors experience challenge, not obstruction

SG-09 Collect an optional visitor self-report at session end

The system shall offer the visitor a short, always-skippable rating and open-feedback prompt when their session ends, and record whatever they choose to answer.
Success criteria (qualitative): A visitor can leave a rating, leave open feedback, leave both, or leave neither, without any of those choices affecting how the session itself concludes.
Satisfies: BG-04 Produce a record of public interactions as secondary
Rationale: Same secondary, non-rigorous status as the rest of BG-04 Produce a record of public interactions as secondary — a self-report gathered in passing from an unbriefed visitor, not BQR-04 Exhibition material is never mistaken for evaluation's formal evaluation sessions.

System architecture

Architecture overview

Architecture style: Client-server, three participants. Two thin browser clients and one stateful backend, with all logic and all credentials held server-side.

Key architecture principles

AP-01 All logic lives in the backend; clients are presentation only

Rationale: The clients run on devices the project does not control, in a public setting. Placing generation, moderation, rate limiting, or session policy in a client would expose the API credential and make every rule editable by anyone with developer tools.
Implications: Rules out client-side calls to the language provider. Any behaviour change ships as a backend change, not a client change.
Satisfies: SG-04 Run unattended and absorb misuse without operator

AP-02 Polling, not push

The projection asks the backend for current material on a fixed interval rather than holding an open connection.
Rationale: A projection refreshing every few seconds is indistinguishable from live at exhibition pace. A persistent connection is one more thing to keep alive across a multi-day unattended run, with reconnection logic that only manifests as a fault when nobody is watching.
Implications: Rules out server-sent events and websockets. Accepts a few seconds of latency between a turn completing and its appearance on the wall.
Satisfies: SG-04 Run unattended and absorb misuse without operator, SG-06 Never present an empty surface

AP-03 Session identity travels in the URL; no accounts, no identity cookies

Rationale: An identifier in the address survives reload, which is the specific failure this system is exposed to. Accounts would defeat SG-01 Start a session from a personal device with no, and identity cookies add a consent surface for no benefit.
Implications: Session identifiers are guessable-resistant but not secret. Rules out storing anything in a session that would be harmful if the URL were shared. Rules out any per-visitor authentication.
Satisfies: SG-01 Start a session from a personal device with no, SG-03 Survive interruption of the visitor’s session

AP-04 One file-backed datastore, held inside the backend element

Rationale: Exhibition-scale volume does not warrant a database server. A single file is copied to back up and inspected with one command, which matters when the maintainer is not on site.
Implications: Rules out concurrent write scaling. Accepts that the store has no independent design surface, so no separate element design document exists for it.
Satisfies: SG-04 Run unattended and absorb misuse without operator, SG-05 Record every interaction with its origin fixed at

AP-05 Moderation gates storage, not a per-session display flag

Content assessed as unsuitable is rejected before it is ever written down: no exchange is stored, no turn is consumed, and the Sparring partner is never called. The visitor edits the contribution and resubmits; the session itself is not ended.
Rationale: An earlier version of this decision ended the participant’s session outright on a moderation hit, on the reasoning that a defensive stance was warranted even at the cost of a visible, unexplained interruption. Built and tested, that traded one problem for a worse one: it gives the visitor no way to recover from a false positive, and — per AP-07 Attempts to argue the Sparring partner out of its — a visitor pressuring the Sparring partner is supposed to keep going, not be cut off. Rejecting the single turn and letting the visitor fix it protects the public surface just as well without punishing engagement the piece exists to produce.
Implications: Moderation runs before the provider call, immediately after the length check (same reasoning: cheapest rejection path, no cost incurred). No is_appropriate ruling is stored against the session, because there is nothing to rule on after the fact — a stored exchange has already passed.
Satisfies: SG-07 Gate public projection at the moment of submission

AP-06 Pilot and live material share one storage and display path, separated by an origin flag

Rationale: A second path for pilot content would need its own rendering and its own failure modes. One path with one discriminating field is less code and makes the separation checkable.
Implications: Requires origin to be set at creation and never inferred. Any extraction of exhibition material must filter on it explicitly.
Satisfies: SG-05 Record every interaction with its origin fixed at, SG-06 Never present an empty surface

AP-07 Attempts to argue the Sparring partner out of its stance are in scope

Content filtering addresses profanity, personal information, and hateful, harassing, threatening, or sexual content targeting a real person or group. It does not address visitors trying to make the system abandon its position.
Rationale: Whether system-placed friction holds against a motivated user is the question the installation exists to expose. Filtering those attempts would remove the observation the piece is built to produce.
Implications: Rules out treating persuasion, pressure, or instruction-style attacks on the Sparring stance as abuse. Requires the moderation function to distinguish adversarial-but-engaged input aimed at the Sparring partner from harmful content aimed at a real person, and this distinction must be stated in that function’s specification rather than left to a wordlist.
Satisfies: SG-05 Record every interaction with its origin fixed at, SG-08 Hold a pedagogical position under sustained

Lesson from testing: stating the adversarial-vs-target distinction was not sufficient on its own. The classification instruction must also state what the exercise’s subject matter actually is — without that, the classifier has nothing to check arguing-the-exercise against, so the subject matter has to be explicit alongside the target-based carve-out, not just the one that looks like the harder problem.

System elements

User types

UT-01 Participating visitor

An exhibition visitor who chooses to interact. No prior briefing, no expertise in agentic AI assumed, attention measured in minutes.
Primary goals: Find out what the piece does. Get a response to something they actually care about. Understand why the system is not simply answering them.
Interacts with: SE-01 Input client
Represents: VCA-01 Participating visitor

UT-02 Observing visitor

Present in the space, not interacting. May be accompanying a participant, waiting, or passing through.
Primary goals: Understand what the piece is without committing to it. Decide whether to participate.
Interacts with: SE-02 Display client (viewing only, no input)
Represents: VCA-02 Observing visitor

UT-03 Operator

The thesis author. Seeds pilot material before the exhibition and intervenes only when something has failed.
Primary goals: Populate the installation before opening. Extract exhibition material afterwards. Diagnose a fault quickly and remotely.
Interacts with: SE-03 Backend service
Represents: VCA-03 Thesis author / exhibitor

Software elements

SE-01 Input client (Web-application)

The surface a visitor reaches by scanning the code, running in their own mobile browser.
Key responsibilities: Presents the consent decision before any input is possible. Accepts and submits visitor contributions. Displays the session’s exchanges to its own participant. Restores an interrupted session from the identifier in the address. Signals that a response is being produced. Reveals a generated visitor alias and avatar once consent is recorded, and lets the visitor deliberately start a new session.
Interacts with: UT-01 Participating visitor, SE-03 Backend service, HE-01 Visitor smartphone
Realises: VCA-04 The installation
Satisfies: SG-01 Start a session from a personal device with no, SG-03 Survive interruption of the visitor’s session

SE-02 Display client (Web-application)

The projected surface, running unattended in a browser on the projection machine.
Key responsibilities: Requests current display material at a fixed interval. Renders exchanges with the scenario that gives them context. Gives prominence to recent material and recedes older material. Fits content to the projection without overflow. Labels each item with a generated visitor alias so exchanges from concurrent sessions are distinguishable.
Interacts with: UT-02 Observing visitor, SE-03 Backend service, HE-02 Projection setup
Realises: VCA-04 The installation
Satisfies: SG-02 Make turn-taking readable from across the room, SG-06 Never present an empty surface

SE-03 Backend service (Backend-service)

The single stateful component. Holds the datastore internally (see AP-04 One file-backed datastore, held inside the backend), so no separate database element is identified.
Key responsibilities: Issues and resolves session identifiers. Produces Sparring responses by calling PE-01 Language generation API. Assesses contributions for projection suitability. Enforces request rate, input size, and session length limits. Persists sessions and exchanges with their origin. Assembles the material the projection displays. Accepts pilot material from the operator.
Interacts with: SE-01 Input client, SE-02 Display client, SE-04 System prompt, PE-01 Language generation API, UT-03 Operator, HE-03 Hosted server
Realises: VCA-04 The installation
Satisfies: SG-01 Start a session from a personal device with no, SG-04 Run unattended and absorb misuse without operator, SG-05 Record every interaction with its origin fixed at, SG-06 Never present an empty surface, SG-07 Gate public projection at the moment of submission
Constrained by: SC-01 PHP with a file-backed store on a single hosted, SC-04 Generation is rate-limited and metered by the provider

SE-04 System prompt (Other — supplies content, no runtime interface of its own)

The instruction set that governs every Sparring response — identity, voice, pedagogical mechanism, and domain grounding — supplied as input content to SE-03’s outbound call rather than run as a component of its own.
Key responsibilities: Defines the Sparring persona and behavioral rules. Specifies the four-stage reasoning engine run on every turn. Specifies the closed Socratic/Sparring move vocabulary. Grounds responses in the FH Dortmund Digital Design curriculum and named design-theory traditions. Specifies boundary-object handling and edge-case behavior.
Interacts with: SE-03 Backend service
Realises: VCA-04 The installation
Satisfies: SG-08 Hold a pedagogical position under sustained

Hardware elements

HE-01 Visitor smartphone

Procurement: Assumed-existing
Managed by: The visitor
Runs: SE-01 Input client
Characteristics: Any current mobile browser with camera-based code scanning. No minimum specification beyond this.

HE-02 Projection setup

Projector and the machine driving it, running a browser in kiosk mode.
Procurement: Supplied by Fachbereich Informatik at FH Dortmund.
Managed by: The operator.
Location: Fixed in the exhibition space. Throw distance and surface determine legible type size, which is why SG-02 Make turn-taking readable from across the room's acceptance criteria remain qualitative until tested in place.
Runs: SE-02 Display client
Characteristics: One projector, one display machine, wired network preferred over wireless for an unattended multi-day run.

HE-03 Hosted server

Procurement: Managed-service
Managed by: Cloud provider
Location: EU region.
Runs: SE-01 Input client, SE-02 Display client, SE-03 Backend service
Characteristics: Smallest generally available instance. Public HTTPS endpoint with a valid certificate, which is required because visitors reach it by scanning a code with a phone camera.

Partner elements

PE-01 Language generation API

Provider: Anthropic (default); OpenAI or an OpenAI-compatible local server, operator-selected
Partner type: Third-party-API
Dependency: Critical. The installation produces no Sparring response without it, and there is no degraded mode that preserves the piece’s purpose.
Used by: SE-03 Backend service

Integration detail (authentication, request format, error taxonomy, retry policy) is specified in SE-03 Backend service's outbound technical interfaces, not here.

Not encoded above, to keep it readable — see the alternative flows list for these: SA-01-1 (declining retention/projection is an opt-out, not a branch in the call chain), SA-01-5 (turn limit reached), SA-01-6 (reload resumes an existing session, skipping consent), SA-01-7 (declining the terms themselves, which — unlike the other opt-outs — does end the flow), SA-01-8 (extending at the turn limit instead of ending).

  • SSt-01-1 UT-01 Participating visitor scans the code and SE-01 Input client opens without a session identifier. (Performed by: UT-01 Participating visitor; Affects: SE-01 Input client)

  • SSt-01-2 SE-01 Input client requests a new session from SE-03 Backend service and receives an identifier, which it places in the address. (Performed by: SE-01 Input client; Affects: SE-03 Backend service)

  • SSt-01-3 SE-01 Input client presents the consent decision — agreement to take part, plus independent retention and projection choices — and holds the input surface until UT-01 Participating visitor responds. (Performed by: SE-01 Input client; Affects: UT-01 Participating visitor)

  • SSt-01-4 UT-01 Participating visitor submits a contribution to SE-03 Backend service through SE-01 Input client. (Performed by: UT-01 Participating visitor; Affects: SE-01 Input client, SE-03 Backend service)

  • SSt-01-5 SE-03 Backend service checks the request against its rate and size limits. (Performed by: SE-03 Backend service)

  • SSt-01-6 SE-03 Backend service assesses the contribution for projection suitability. An unsuitable contribution is rejected here — nothing is stored, no turn is consumed, and the flow does not continue to SSt-01-7. (Performed by: SE-03 Backend service)

  • SSt-01-7 SE-03 Backend service requests a Sparring response from PE-01 Language generation API, supplying the session’s prior exchanges. (Performed by: SE-03 Backend service; Affects: PE-01 Language generation API)

  • SSt-01-8 PE-01 Language generation API returns the response to SE-03 Backend service. (Performed by: PE-01 Language generation API; Affects: SE-03 Backend service)

  • SSt-01-9 SE-03 Backend service stores the visitor contribution and the response as one exchange and returns the response to SE-01 Input client. (Performed by: SE-03 Backend service; Affects: SE-01 Input client)

  • SSt-01-10 SE-01 Input client presents the response to UT-01 Participating visitor and reopens the input surface. (Performed by: SE-01 Input client; Affects: UT-01 Participating visitor)

  • SSt-01-11 The exchange becomes available to SE-02 Display client on its next request, if the session is eligible for projection. (Performed by: SE-03 Backend service; Affects: SE-02 Display client)

Alternative flows

  • SA-01-1 (extends SSt-01-3) UT-01 Participating visitor declines retention, projection, or both. SE-03 Backend service records the decision(s) and the session proceeds normally either way — these are independent opt-outs, not preconditions of participating. Outcome: Alternative-success.

  • SA-01-2 (extends SSt-01-5) The request exceeds the rate or size limit. SE-03 Backend service rejects it and SE-01 Input client tells UT-01 Participating visitor plainly. The session remains usable. Outcome: Resume.

  • SA-01-3 (extends SSt-01-6) The contribution is assessed as unsuitable for projection. SE-03 Backend service rejects the turn and returns that condition to SE-01 Input client, which tells UT-01 Participating visitor to edit and resubmit. Nothing is stored, no turn is consumed, and the session is otherwise unaffected. Outcome: Resume.

  • SA-01-4 (extends SSt-01-7) PE-01 Language generation API is unavailable or fails after retries. SE-03 Backend service returns a failure and SE-01 Input client states that the installation cannot respond. Outcome: Terminate.

  • SA-01-5 (extends SSt-01-4) The session has reached its turn limit. SE-03 Backend service rejects the contribution and SE-01 Input client presents the session as complete, offering the visitor a choice: extend the session (SA-01-8) or go to the optional session-evaluation prompt (SG-09 Collect an optional visitor self-report at session). Outcome: Terminate.

  • SA-01-6 (extends SSt-01-1) SE-01 Input client opens with a session identifier already in the address after a reload. It fetches the existing session from SE-03 Backend service and resumes from SSt-01-4, skipping the consent decision. Outcome: Alternative-success.

  • SA-01-7 (extends SSt-01-3) UT-01 Participating visitor declines to agree to the terms of participation. Unlike retention and projection, this is not an opt-out the session can proceed without — the input surface stays unusable. Outcome: Terminate.

  • SA-01-8 (extends SA-01-5) At the turn limit UT-01 Participating visitor chooses to keep going rather than end. SE-03 Backend service lifts the session’s turn ceiling by another allowance and SE-01 Input client re-enables the input surface. The choice is offered again each time the raised ceiling is reached. Outcome: Resume.

SSc-02 The projection presents current material

Detail level: Narrative
Achieves: SG-02 Make turn-taking readable from across the room, SG-06 Never present an empty surface
Realises: BP-02 Recent sessions are shown to the room

SE-02 Display client requests display material from SE-03 Backend service on a fixed interval. SE-03 Backend service assembles the most recent sessions eligible for projection, each reduced to a scenario statement and a bounded selection of its exchanges, and returns them ordered by recency. SE-02 Display client compares the result against what it is currently showing and updates only what has changed, so that unchanged material does not visibly redraw. New exchanges take prominence; older ones recede. The exact treatment of prominence and recession is TO BE REFINED and is specified as an open decision in SE-02 Display client's element design.

Alternative flows

  • SA-02-1 (extends the assembly step) Fewer eligible live sessions exist than the projection can show. SE-03 Backend service fills the remainder with pilot-origin material, ordered by recency within its own group. Outcome: Alternative-success.

  • SA-02-2 (extends the request step) SE-02 Display client cannot reach SE-03 Backend service. It continues to display the last material it successfully retrieved rather than clearing the surface, and retries on the next interval. Outcome: Resume.

SSc-03 The operator seeds pilot material

Detail level: Narrative
Achieves: SG-05 Record every interaction with its origin fixed at, SG-06 Never present an empty surface

Before the exhibition opens, UT-03 Operator supplies transcripts from pilot sessions to SE-03 Backend service in a structured file. SE-03 Backend service creates the corresponding sessions and exchanges with their origin fixed as pilot, derives a scenario statement for each, and marks them eligible for projection. This path performs no generation and does not call PE-01 Language generation API, because the responses already exist in the transcripts.

Alternative flows

  • SA-03-1 (extends the creation step) A transcript is malformed. SE-03 Backend service rejects that transcript, reports which one, and continues with the remainder. Outcome: Resume.

Quality requirements

SQR-01 A Sparring response arrives, or its absence is visible (Performance)

Applies to: SE-01 Input client, SE-03 Backend service, PE-01 Language generation API
Acceptance criteria: SE-01 Input client presents an unambiguous in-progress state within 300 ms of submission and maintains it until a response or failure arrives. SE-03 Backend service abandons a generation request after a bounded interval rather than leaving the visitor with an indefinite wait. The bound is 20 seconds (GENERATION_TIMEOUT_SECONDS, config.php).
Supports: BQR-01 Visitors experience challenge, not obstruction
Rationale: On a phone in a gallery, several seconds of nothing is read as broken. The in-progress state is not decoration.

SQR-02 The installation recovers without a person present (Availability)

Applies to: SE-02 Display client, SE-03 Backend service, HE-03 Hosted server
Acceptance criteria (qualitative): SE-02 Display client recovers from a backend outage on its own next interval with no reload. SE-03 Backend service restarts automatically after a process failure. No fault requires physical access to HE-02 Projection setup to clear.
Supports: BQR-02 The piece is available whenever the room is open

SQR-03 The projection is readable at exhibition viewing distance (Usability)

Applies to: SE-02 Display client, HE-02 Projection setup
Acceptance criteria (qualitative): A complete exchange is readable by a standing observer at the room’s typical viewing distance. Verified in place on the actual projector before opening, not in a browser window.
Supports: BQR-01 Visitors experience challenge, not obstruction

SQR-04 Credentials and quota are protected from the public (Security)

Applies to: SE-03 Backend service
Acceptance criteria: The provider credential is never transmitted to any client. Request volume is limited per origin over a rolling window (10 requests / 60 seconds — RATE_LIMIT_MAX_REQUESTS, RATE_LIMIT_WINDOW_SECONDS, config.php). Contribution length is bounded (600 characters — CONTRIBUTION_MAX_CHARS), except a curated opening prompt (OPENING_PROMPTS, config.php), which is vetted copy rather than visitor-typed text and so is exempt. Session length is bounded per allowance (16 exchanges — TURN_ALLOWANCE); at the limit the visitor may extend for another allowance (SA-01-8), each extension a fresh bounded segment rather than an unbounded session.
Externally sourced: Yes (provider terms and cost exposure rather than a business quality requirement)

SQR-05 Origin is fixed at creation and never inferred (Data-integrity)

Applies to: SE-03 Backend service
Acceptance criteria (qualitative): Every session carries pilot or live from the moment it is created. No process assigns or corrects origin afterwards. Extraction of exhibition material filters on the stored value.
Supports: BQR-04 Exhibition material is never mistaken for evaluation

SQR-06 Public projection is governed separately from retention (Compliance)

Applies to: SE-03 Backend service, SE-02 Display client
Acceptance criteria (qualitative): Display eligibility, retention consent, and agreement to take part are stored as three distinct properties, each recorded independently. A session may be retained and not projected, or projected and not retained, in any combination. Display eligibility is set directly from the visitor’s projection consent at decision time — suitability is enforced earlier, at write time (AP-05 Moderation gates storage, not a per-session display/SG-07 Gate public projection at the moment of submission), so nothing reaches storage that projection consent alone would need to filter back out.
Supports: BQR-03 Nothing identifying a visitor reaches the projection

SQR-07 The installation degrades visibly rather than silently (Reliability, recorded under Availability)

Applies to: SE-01 Input client, SE-02 Display client
Acceptance criteria (qualitative): Every failure the visitor can encounter produces a plain statement of what happened. No failure presents as an unresponsive interface. This includes routing failures (unmatched path) and uncaught infrastructure faults on a page request, not only the failure conditions SE-01 Input client/SE-02 Display client raise themselves.
Supports: BQR-01 Visitors experience challenge, not obstruction

SC-05 Retention requires consent; projection requires consent, and only ever carries content that already passed suitability (Legal-regulatory)

Source: BC-02 Public user-generated content is subject to data.
Applies to: SE-01 Input client, SE-03 Backend service
Acceptance criteria (qualitative): No session is retained past the exhibition without a recorded decision. No session is projected without a recorded projection decision, and nothing unsuitable is ever available to project in the first place — suitability is enforced at write time (AP-05 Moderation gates storage, not a per-session display), not as a second gate applied to already-stored content. Neither retention nor projection consent may be bundled with the precondition of taking part at all (BC-02 Public user-generated content is subject to data).
Consequence: Rules out a single consent switch governing both retention and projection, and rules out treating "may I participate" and "may my content be kept/shown" as the same decision.
Refines: BC-02 Public user-generated content is subject to data
